\begin{abstract}
Students and researchers navigating technical literature frequently need both
canonical concept grounding (linking a question to a structured ontology node and
its prerequisite neighborhood) and verifiable passage provenance, while avoiding
hallucinated citations when a topic lies outside the indexed corpus. We demonstrate
\textbf{Archipelago}, an interactive, graph-assisted academic research workspace
built over a frozen corpus of 775 passages and 610 AI/ML and Information Retrieval
concepts. Rather than positioning graph traversal as a replacement for flat passage
rankers, Archipelago focuses on (i) concept-level resolution to canonical ontology
nodes, (ii) interactive graph neighborhood inspection alongside direct PDF page
linking, and (iii) explicit abstention when queries fall outside the indexed
knowledge boundary. On a frozen 50-query held-out benchmark (38 supported, 12
out-of-domain), Archipelago achieves 68.42\% top-1 concept resolution (26/38) versus
42.11\% for BM25 and 36.84\% for \texttt{snowflake-arctic-embed-m}, while achieving
12/12 (100\%) abstention with zero fabricated citations or graph edges on
out-of-domain queries. We also report candid negative findings: on provisional
algorithmic passage qrels, BM25 and neural dense retrieval (MRR 0.7500) outperform
Archipelago's heuristic graph-assisted passage ranking (MRR 0.4865), runtime exact
triple extraction achieves 0.0 F1 due to a predicate-schema gap, and an author-only
citation audit finds 29.17\% strict claim support (7/24) with 37.50\% neighborhood
dilution (9/24). Independent human relevance judging over a 731-item system-blinded
top-5 pool is specified and pending.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Interactive scholarly search and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems
\cite{lewis2020rag} commonly return flat lists of retrieved text chunks or fluent
LLM summaries that obscure how technical concepts depend on one another and fail
silently when a query falls outside the indexed collection. Conversely, flat
lexical rankers such as BM25 \cite{robertson2009bm25} and dense bi-encoders such as
DPR \cite{karpukhin2020dpr} excel at passage-level ranking but always return top-$k$
passages even for completely unsupported domains, providing no structural abstention
signal.

We demonstrate \textbf{Archipelago}, an academic research workspace that pairs a
concept-anchored knowledge graph (610 concept nodes, 775 deduplicated source
passages) with an interactive chat interface, graph inspector, and page-level PDF
viewer. Our design explicitly separates \emph{concept resolution and boundary
abstention} from \emph{full-corpus passage ranking}. In keeping with rigorous IR
demonstration standards, we report both verified strengths and candid failure modes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Concept Resolution:} Archipelago resolves 26/38 (68.42\%) supported
        held-out queries to their canonical concept node, outperforming BM25 (16/38,
        42.11\%) and zero-shot neural dense matching (14/38, 36.84\%) at 22.73\,ms
        mean in-process latency.
  \item \textbf{Boundary Abstention:} On all 12 held-out out-of-domain queries
        (e.g., Spanner TrueTime, CRISPR-Cas9, Navier-Stokes), Archipelago returns
        \texttt{anchor\_concept=None}, \texttt{render\_graph=False}, and zero
        indexed corpus passage citations (12/12, 100\%; 11/12 return zero total
        citations while \texttt{Q45} surfaces one Pearson catalog book metadata
        pointer with zero graph edges).
  \item \textbf{Transparent Limitations:} We explicitly disclose where Archipelago
        does \emph{not} outperform baselines---flat passage retrieval (BM25/neural
        MRR 0.7500 vs.\ Archipelago 0.4865 on provisional algorithmic qrels), exact
        typed relation extraction (TP=0/18), and neighborhood citation dilution
        (37.50\% spurious citations in an author-only 24-citation audit).
\end{itemize}

\section{System Architecture}
Archipelago comprises three decoupled services and a frozen knowledge fixture
(\texttt{okf\_graph.json}, 610 concepts, 775 source passages $\ge 40$ chars):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Concept Resolution \& Abstention Engine (\texttt{host\_inference}):}
        Maps incoming user queries to a canonical concept node via lexical/alias
        matching and embedding similarity over concept descriptors, applying an
        abstention boundary that suppresses graph rendering and citations when no
        indexed anchor meets threshold.
  \item \textbf{Knowledge Graph Neighborhood Layer:} Traverses directed edges in
        \texttt{okf\_graph.json} across three runtime predicates---\texttt{REQUIRES},
        \texttt{RELATED}, and \texttt{UNLOCKS}---surfacing prerequisites, unlocked
        topics, and related concepts alongside canonical \texttt{doc\_id::chunk\_id}
        passage citations.
  \item \textbf{Interactive Research Workspace UI:} Presents three synchronized
        panes: (a) grounded chat synthesis with clickable inline citations, (b) an
        interactive subgraph inspector centered on the resolved anchor concept, and
        (c) an authorized PDF page viewer and scholarly API discovery integrations
        (OpenAlex, Crossref, arXiv, and institutional Koha OPAC snapshot).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Demonstration Walkthrough}
At the SIGIR demonstration booth, attendees interact with three live scenarios
(5 minutes total) using verified runtime behaviors:

\begin{description}
  \item[Scenario 1: Transformer Concept Grounding.] Querying \emph{``What is
        Transformer?''} resolves anchor \texttt{transformer}, displays
        \texttt{REQUIRES}/\texttt{RELATED}/\texttt{UNLOCKS} neighbors (including
        \texttt{matrix\_multiplication}, \texttt{self\_attention}, and
        \texttt{linear\_algebra}), and links rank-1 citation
        \cite{vaswani2017attention} (p.\,2) in the page viewer. Follow-up
        \emph{``How does self-attention relate to the Transformer?''} anchors on
        \texttt{self\_attention} with two Vaswani et al.\ passages (pp.\,5, 10),
        while also illustrating our honest disclosure of a legacy inverted
        \texttt{REQUIRES} edge in the static graph fixture.
  \item[Scenario 2: RAG and Dense Retrieval Neighborhoods.] Querying \emph{``What is
        Retrieval-Augmented Generation?''} anchors on \texttt{rag} with rank-1
        citation to Lewis et al.\ \cite{lewis2020rag} (p.\,4), while \emph{``How
        does DPR compare to BM25 for passage retrieval?''} anchors on
        \texttt{retrieval} and surfaces \texttt{dpr},
        \texttt{dense\_passage\_retriever}, and \texttt{bm25} in the concept
        neighborhood. Attendees can inspect both grounded citations and lower-ranked
        neighborhood dilution directly in the evidence drawer.
  \item[Scenario 3: Honest Out-of-Domain Abstention.] Querying \emph{``Explain
        Spanner TrueTime and its prerequisites.''} triggers boundary abstention:
        \texttt{anchor\_concept=None}, \texttt{render\_graph=False}, and zero
        citations, preventing fabricated prerequisite chains or broken PDF links.
\end{description}

\section{Empirical Evaluation \& Audit}
All experiments use frozen, SHA-256-locked artifacts in
\texttt{research/d5\_sigir\_evaluation/}: a 15-query development split
(\texttt{queries\_dev\_15.json}) and a 50-query held-out split
(\texttt{queries\_heldout\_50.json}: 20 core concept, 18 relational, 12
out-of-domain abstention) over the 775-passage, 610-concept corpus. Zero query IDs
and zero normalized query strings overlap between dev and held-out splits (7 target
concepts overlap due to the compact AI/ML/IR domain; zero tuning was performed on
held-out queries). We compare Archipelago against BM25Okapi ($k_1=1.5, b=0.75$),
a 384-dim BLAKE2b feature-hashed vector baseline, and pretrained neural dense
retrieval (\texttt{Snowflake/snowflake-arctic-embed-m} \cite{merrick2024arctic},
pinned revision \texttt{fc74610d18462d218e312aa986ec5c8a75a98152}, 768-dim, CUDA).

\subsection{Experiment A: Concept Resolution \& Abstention}
Table~\ref{tab:concept} reports top-1 canonical concept resolution on the 38
supported queries and abstention accuracy on the 12 unsupported queries. Archipelago
achieves 68.42\% supported accuracy (+26.31 pp over BM25; +31.58 pp over
Snowflake-Arctic) and 100\% abstention (12/12) with zero citations on unsupported
queries. The 12 supported misses stem from unindexed ontology nodes (e.g.,
\texttt{cross\_encoder}, \texttt{dual\_encoder}), alias mismatches
(\texttt{dpr} vs.\ \texttt{dense\_passage\_retrieval}), and compound multi-word
phrasings.

\subsection{Experiment C \& Grounding Audits}
\paragraph{Exact Runtime Relationship Prediction.}
Evaluating exact \texttt{(source, predicate, destination)} triple equality over live
runtime graph edges (\texttt{engine.graph.out}/\texttt{inn}) across the 18 relational
queries yields \textbf{TP=0}, Precision=0.0, Recall=0.0 (over 2 assessable queries),
and F1=0.0. Thirteen of 18 queries request predicates absent from the runtime schema
(\texttt{USES}, \texttt{CONTRASTS\_WITH}, \texttt{APPLIES\_TO}), three resolve no
anchor (\texttt{Q29}, \texttt{Q30}, \texttt{Q34}), and the two assessable
\texttt{REQUIRES} queries (\texttt{Q22}, \texttt{Q37}) do not surface their expected
triples. We therefore make zero claims of typed relation-extraction accuracy.

\paragraph{Citation Grounding \& Edge Provenance (Author-Only).}
An author-only audit (Pratay Karali; \texttt{independent\_peer\_review=false}) of
24 live citations across 5 representative queries (\texttt{Q01}, \texttt{Q08},
\texttt{Q21}, \texttt{Q23}, \texttt{Q39}) found \textbf{7/24 (29.17\%)} fully
supported, 6/24 (25.00\%) partially supported, 1/24 unsupported, \textbf{9/24
(37.50\%)} irrelevant or spurious due to broad neighborhood citation expansion, and
1/24 unassessable. Similarly, 45 graph-slice edges (40 verified, 5 quarantined) were
audited solely by the primary author. \todo{INDEPENDENT-AUDIT: Report independent

\section{Limitations, Ethics, and Reproducibility}
Archipelago's current limitations are explicitly surfaced in the UI and evaluation
artifacts: (1) passage qrels are algorithmic pending completion of our 731-item
system-blinded pooled top-5 human annotation and third-party adjudication protocol
(explicitly single-blinded to system and rank, not clinical double-blind);
(2) broad neighborhood citation collection introduces 37.50\% spurious citations
beyond the primary anchor citation; (3) the runtime graph schema supports only
\texttt{REQUIRES}, \texttt{RELATED}, and \texttt{UNLOCKS}; and (4) copyrighted
textbooks are never stored in full---only open-access research papers, authorized
metadata, and institutional OPAC/eLibrary handoffs are indexed. All frozen splits,
SHA-256 manifests, pinned model revisions, automated integrity tests, and blank
human worksheets are released in the project repository for full reproducibility.
