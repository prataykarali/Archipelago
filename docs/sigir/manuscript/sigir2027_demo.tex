\begin{abstract}
Students, instructors, and researchers navigating technical literature require
canonical concept grounding---linking a question to a structured curriculum ontology
node and its prerequisite neighborhood---alongside verifiable page-level PDF
provenance, without hallucinated citations on out-of-collection queries. We
demonstrate \textbf{Archipelago}, a deployed, graph-assisted academic research
workspace built over a frozen fixture of 610 AI/ML and Information Retrieval concepts
and 775 deduplicated source passages. Rather than positioning graph traversal as a
replacement for flat passage rankers, Archipelago separates \emph{concept resolution
and boundary abstention} from \emph{source provenance and live scholarly discovery}.
On a frozen 50-query held-out benchmark (38 in-scope, 12 out-of-corpus), Archipelago
achieves 68.42\% top-1 concept resolution (26/38; 95\% Wilson CI $[52.5\%, 80.9\%]$),
significantly outperforming BM25 (42.11\%, 16/38; exact McNemar $p=0.0020$),
\path{snowflake-arctic-embed-m} (36.84\%, 14/38; $p=0.0005$), and feature hashing
(7.89\%, 3/38; $p<0.0001$). On out-of-corpus queries, Archipelago achieves 12/12
(100\%) concept, graph, and passage abstention (11/12 zero total citations, with
\texttt{Q45} surfacing one Pearson catalog book metadata pointer). We also report
candid negative findings: on provisional algorithmic passage qrels ($N=37$), flat
BM25 and neural retrieval (MRR 0.7500) outperform heuristic graph-assisted passage
ranking (MRR 0.4865), runtime exact triple prediction yields 0/18 true positives due
to a 3-predicate schema gap, and a preliminary author-attributed 24-citation audit
found 29.17\% strict claim support (7/24) and 37.50\% neighborhood dilution (9/24),
motivating our deterministic $\le 3$-citation anchor selector.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem, Target Users \& IR Novelty}
Graduate students, curriculum designers, and literature reviewers exploring technical
domains such as deep learning and information retrieval face a structural mismatch
between standard retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) interfaces \cite{lewis2020rag}
and curriculum-grounded study. Flat lexical rankers such as BM25
\cite{robertson2009bm25} and dense bi-encoders such as DPR \cite{karpukhin2020dpr}
and Arctic-Embed \cite{merrick2024arctic} excel at passage-level similarity but
always return top-$k$ text chunks even when a query asks about an out-of-collection
topic (e.g., distributed consensus or gene editing), offering no structural
abstention gate or prerequisite topology. Conversely, unconstrained LLM chat
assistants synthesize fluent prose that frequently conflates \emph{source provenance}
(whether a cited PDF page and chunk actually exist in the repository) with
\emph{claim entailment} (whether the passage proves the generated sentence), or
dilutes primary foundational citations with peripheral neighborhood papers.

\textbf{Archipelago} addresses these gaps for academic and institutional library
users through six demonstrable interactive IR contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Concept-Anchored Graph Navigation:} Resolves natural-language
        questions to canonical nodes in a 610-concept ontology before exposing 1-hop
        \texttt{REQUIRES}, \texttt{RELATED}, and \texttt{UNLOCKS} neighborhoods.
  \item \textbf{Explicit Coverage Boundary \& Abstention Gate:} Suppresses graph
        rendering (\path{render_graph=False}) and corpus passage citations when no
        indexed concept meets grounding thresholds (12/12 held-out abstention).
  \item \textbf{Provenance vs.\ Claim-Support Separation:} Deterministic evidence
        selection (Issue~\#11) verifies canonical \path{doc_id::chunk_id}
        provenance while explicitly labeling claim support as
        \path{not_assessed} rather than overclaiming automated entailment.
  \item \textbf{Click-Through Canonical PDF Verification:} Streams exact source pages
        (\path{/read}, verified \texttt{\%PDF-} byte streams) alongside the graph.
  \item \textbf{Live Scholarly Discovery \& XLSX Structuring:} Queries OpenAlex,
        Crossref, and arXiv (\path{/research}) with per-user isolated persistence
        and 4-sheet OOXML \texttt{.xlsx} export containing live DOI hyperlinks.
  \item \textbf{OPAC Freshness Disclosure:} Bridges curated e-books and 109 Koha
        catalog records (\path{/library}) with explicit snapshot staleness
        indicators (\path{koha_stale: true}) and live \texttt{biblionumber} handoffs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Verified Architecture \& Live System}
Figure~\ref{fig:arch} illustrates Archipelago's end-to-end runtime pipeline,
deployed and verified live at \url{https://archipelago-blond.vercel.app}
(production deployment \path{dpl_8QiQvF79qZLQS9oj5xaobxurDarY}, code release SHA
\texttt{1785846a}, full commit \path{1785846a49db27d5931beb596cf64bbbae778f3c},
distinct graph fingerprint \texttt{9818ac0e8fd3}). The backend loads a frozen
knowledge graph fixture (\path{okf_graph.json}: 610 concepts, 775 deduplicated
passages $\ge 40$ chars, and 40 Pearson institutional catalog metadata records).

When an authenticated user submits a query to \path{POST /api/chat}, the
\textbf{Concept Resolver} (\path{host_inference/engine/answer.py}) combines
normalized phrase matching, alias lookup, and descriptor similarity. If no concept
clears the grounding gate, the \textbf{Coverage / Abstention Gate} intercepts the
request (\path{GUARDRAIL_INTERCEPT} / \path{no_verified_concept_match}),
setting \path{anchor_concept=None}, \path{render_graph=False}, and
\path{citations=[]}. When an anchor resolves, the \textbf{Deterministic Evidence
Selector} (\path{host_inference/engine/evidence_selection.py}) ranks live sources
on the anchor node using primary-paper hints (e.g., Vaswani et al.\
\cite{vaswani2017attention} for \texttt{transformer}, Devlin et al.\
\cite{devlin2019bert} for \texttt{bert}, Lewis et al.\ \cite{lewis2020rag} for
\texttt{rag}, Karpukhin et al.\ \cite{karpukhin2020dpr} for \texttt{dpr}, Dao et al.\
\cite{dao2022flashattention} for \texttt{flashattention}, Hu et al.\
\cite{hu2022lora} for \path{low_rank_adaptation}, Kwon et al.\
\cite{kwon2023vllm} for \path{paged_attention}, Edge et al.\
\cite{edge2024graphrag} for \path{graph_rag}), filters low-signal bibliography or
end-matter dumps, deduplicates by canonical \path{(doc_id, chunk_id)}, and caps
output at \path{MAX_ANCHOR_CITATIONS = 3}.

\section{Interactive Demonstration Scenarios}
All three booth scenarios (and six end-to-end authenticated journeys J1--J6) were
verified live on production release \texttt{1785846a} via headless Chromium and byte
inspection:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Scenario 1: Grounded Concept Graph \& Original PDF (\texttt{/chat} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{/read}).}
        Submitting \emph{``What is a Transformer?''} (Figure~\ref{fig:ui}) resolves
        anchor \texttt{transformer}, renders its 1-hop prerequisite and unlocked
        subgraph (\texttt{Matrix Multiplication}, \texttt{Attention Mechanism},
        \texttt{Neural Network}), and surfaces 3 canonical Vaswani et al.\
        \cite{vaswani2017attention} citations labeled
        \path{provenance_status="provenance_verified"} and
        \path{claim_support_status="not_assessed"}. Clicking rank-1 citation
        \texttt{S1 (PDF p.2)} opens \path{/read?doc=papers/Vaswani2017_Attention_Is_All_You_Need.pdf&page=2},
        streaming the verified 2,215,244-byte PDF (15 pages) directly to page~2.
        Follow-up relational queries also let attendees inspect where compound phrasing
        or legacy static edges fail transparently.
  \item \textbf{Scenario 2: Live Scholarly Discovery, Dossier \& XLSX Export (\texttt{/research}).}
        Searching \emph{``Dense Passage Retrieval''} retrieves 30 live scholarly works
        from OpenAlex/Crossref, persists the user-isolated run across browser reloads,
        hydrates a 186\,KB structured dossier with resolving DOIs, and exports a
        verified 26,511-byte, 4-sheet \texttt{.xlsx} workbook (\texttt{Start Here},
        \texttt{Papers}, \texttt{Authors}, \texttt{Sources}) containing 103 live
        hyperlinks.
  \item \textbf{Scenario 3: Institutional OPAC Freshness \& Out-of-Corpus Abstention (\texttt{/library}, \texttt{/chat}).}
        In \path{/library}, attendees browse 91 reader-backed e-books and 109 Koha
        holdings displaying explicit snapshot freshness (\path{koha_stale: true},
        timestamped \texttt{2026-11-11}) and open live institutional Koha OPAC
        records (\path{biblionumber=1910}). Returning to \path{/chat}, querying
        \emph{``What is Spanner TrueTime?''} (\texttt{Q39}) triggers
        \path{GUARDRAIL_INTERCEPT} in 0.02\,s with \textbf{0 citations} and
        \textbf{0 graph edges}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Qualified Empirical Evaluation}
All evaluations execute deterministically against SHA-256-locked artifacts in
\path{research/d5_sigir_evaluation/}: a 15-query dev split
(\path{queries_dev_15.json}) and a 50-query held-out split
(\path{queries_heldout_50.json}: 20 core concept, 18 relational, 12 unsupported
abstention) over the 610-concept, 775-passage corpus. We compare Archipelago against
BM25Okapi ($k_1=1.5, b=0.75$), a 384-dim BLAKE2b feature-hashed vector baseline, and
pretrained neural dense retrieval (\path{Snowflake/snowflake-arctic-embed-m}
\cite{merrick2024arctic}, pinned revision \texttt{fc74610d}, 768-dim).

\subsection{Experiment A: Concept Resolution \& Abstention}
Table~\ref{tab:concept} and Figure~\ref{fig:exp_a} report top-1 canonical concept
resolution across the $N=38$ in-scope queries. Archipelago resolves \textbf{26/38
(68.42\%}, 95\% Wilson CI $[52.5\%, 80.9\%]$), outperforming BM25 (16/38, 42.11\%, CI
$[27.9\%, 57.8\%]$), Snowflake-Arctic (14/38, 36.84\%, CI $[23.4\%, 52.7\%]$), and
Feature-Hashed (3/38, 7.89\%, CI $[2.7\%, 20.8\%]$). Reconstructing per-query paired
outcomes shows zero discordant losses for Archipelago ($c=0$): paired exact two-sided
McNemar tests yield $p=0.0020$ vs.\ BM25 ($b=10, c=0$), $p=0.0005$ vs.\
Snowflake-Arctic ($b=12, c=0$), and $p<0.0001$ vs.\ Feature-Hashed ($b=23, c=0$).

On the $N=12$ out-of-corpus queries (\texttt{Q39}--\texttt{Q50}), Figure~\ref{fig:abstention}
separates four verification criteria: Archipelago achieves \textbf{12/12 (100\%},
Wilson CI $[75.8\%, 100.0\%]$) on (1) no resolved concept, (2) no graph rendering,
and (3) zero indexed corpus passage citations, and \textbf{11/12 (91.67\%}, CI
$[64.6\%, 98.5\%]$) on (4) zero total citations because \texttt{Q45} (\emph{``compilers''})
surfaces one Pearson catalog book metadata pointer (\emph{Compilers, 2e},
\path{is_pearson=True}) with zero graph nodes and zero corpus passages.

\subsection{Experiment B: Provisional Passage Ranking}
Figure~\ref{fig:exp_b} and Table~\ref{tab:concept} evaluate top-5 passage ranking
across the $N=37$ queries with non-empty candidate qrels (12 abstention queries and
supported query \texttt{Q19} have zero candidate labels in \path{unjudged_qrels.json}).
\textbf{Mandatory disclosure:} all 445 positive labels in \path{passage_qrels.json}
are \path{ALGORITHMIC_RULE_BASED_CANDIDATE} labels (\path{human_reviewed=false}),
not human judgments. On these provisional qrels, BM25 (MRR 0.7500, nDCG@5 0.6415,
R@5 0.3707, P@5 0.5405; 3.08\,ms) and Snowflake-Arctic (MRR 0.7500, nDCG@5 0.6524,

citation-order decay (MRR 0.4865, nDCG@5 0.2253, R@5 0.0597, P@5 0.1730; 22.73\,ms).
We never claim passage-ranking superiority: Archipelago is a concept-grounding and

\subsection{Failure Taxonomy \& Citation Grounding Audit}
Figure~\ref{fig:taxonomy} categorizes all observed failure modes across the 50
held-out queries:
(i) \textbf{Missing ontology concepts (6/38 supported):} target concepts absent from
\path{okf_graph.json} (\texttt{Q11} \path{scaled_dot_product_attention},
\texttt{Q16} \path{inverted_index}, \texttt{Q17} \path{maximum_inner_product_search},
\texttt{Q18} \path{dual_encoder}, \texttt{Q19}/\texttt{Q33} \path{cross_encoder};
note \texttt{Q12} \path{positional_encoding} is also absent as a node ID but succeeds
via gold concept \texttt{transformer});
(ii) \textbf{Alias/compound phrasing misses (4/38):} concept exists in the ontology
but returns \texttt{None} (\texttt{Q20}/\texttt{Q34} \path{graph_rag},
\texttt{Q29} \path{self_attention}, \texttt{Q30} \path{attention_mechanism});
(iii) \textbf{Incorrect canonical anchoring (2/38):} \texttt{Q22} and \texttt{Q36}
resolve to coarse \path{attention_mechanism} instead of \path{self_attention} /
\texttt{transformer};
(iv) \textbf{Unsupported runtime predicates (13/18 relational):} exact
\texttt{(source, predicate, destination)} triple evaluation yields \textbf{0/18 TP
(F1 = 0.0)} because the runtime graph stores only \texttt{REQUIRES}, \texttt{RELATED},
and \texttt{UNLOCKS}, whereas 13 queries request \texttt{USES} (10),
\path{CONTRASTS_WITH} (2), or \path{APPLIES_TO} (1), 3 resolve no anchor, and 2
assessable \texttt{REQUIRES} queries (\texttt{Q22}, \texttt{Q37}) miss their target triple;
and (v) \textbf{Wrong relation direction (1 quarantined legacy edge):}
\path{self_attention -[REQUIRES]-> transformer} in \path{okf_graph.json}
(quarantined during author review of 45 graph edges: 40 verified, 5 quarantined).

\section{Limitations, Reproducibility \& Impact}
We highlight four methodological limitations and reproducibility safeguards:
(1) \textbf{Sample size \& ontology overlap:} The held-out benchmark is compact
($N=50$: 38 supported, 12 unsupported); while zero query IDs or normalized strings
overlap with the 15-query dev split, 2 dev/test pairs have content-token Jaccard
$\ge 0.40$ and 7 target concepts overlap across the domain ontology (zero tuning was
performed on held-out queries).
(2) \textbf{Baseline threshold calibration:} Baseline abstention cutoffs (BM25 $>2.0$,
Feature-Hashed $>0.35$, Snowflake-Arctic $>0.45$) are fixed heuristic thresholds; formal
operating-point calibration must use dev queries only.
(3) \textbf{Re-blinded human annotation pool:} Because the legacy
\path{worksheets/pool_key_SEALED.json} mapping was committed to public git history,
our D9 protocol issues freshly re-blinded 731-item reviewer packets keyed by an
external private key stored strictly outside the repository, awaiting two independent
human annotators and a third adjudicator ($\kappa_w \ge 0.60$, Krippendorff's
$\alpha \ge 0.67$).
(4) \textbf{Copyright \& artifact locks:} Full textbooks are never stored in the
repository; all 11 frozen evaluation artifacts, SHA-256 locks, figure scripts, and
blank annotation packets are released for full reproducibility.
