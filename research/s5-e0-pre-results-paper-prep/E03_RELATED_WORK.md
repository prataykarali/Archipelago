## 1. Lexical Retrieval vs. Dense Embeddings: The Zero-Shot Generalization and Efficiency Tradeoff

Modern ad-hoc passage retrieval is structured around a well-documented tension between exact lexical matching and learned semantic representation. Under the Probabilistic Relevance Framework (PRF), Robertson and Walker (1994) (`robertson1994some`) derived tractable approximations to the 2-Poisson term-eliteness model, which Robertson and Zaragoza (2009) (`robertson2009probabilistic`) formalized as Okapi BM25 with concave term-frequency saturation ($k_1$) and document-length normalization ($b$). Subsequent empirical re-examinations by Trotman, Puurula, and Burgess (2014) (`trotman2014improvements`) showed that while minor IDF-floor and parameterization variants exist, standard BM25 remains a remarkably resilient, zero-training lexical baseline whose effectiveness is difficult to beat consistently without domain-specific supervision.

Dense passage retrieval emerged to overcome BM25's vocabulary-mismatch ceiling. Reimers and Gurevych (2019) (`reimers2019sentence`) established that Siamese bi-encoder architectures (Sentence-BERT) can compress cross-encoder Transformer semantics into fixed-dimensional vectors amenable to fast inner-product search, and Karpukhin et al. (2020) (`karpukhin2020dense`) demonstrated that dual-encoder Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) trained with in-batch and BM25-mined hard negatives substantially outperforms BM25 on in-domain open-domain QA benchmarks. However, large-scale out-of-domain evaluation on BEIR by Thakur et al. (2021) (`thakur2021beir`) revealed a critical asymmetry: while dense bi-encoders excel on semantically paraphrased queries within their training distribution, **BM25 remains highly competitive—and often stronger—in zero-shot technical, scientific, and entity-heavy domains** where exact terminology, acronyms, and rare identifiers dominate. More recent embedding families such as Snowflake Arctic-Embed (Merrick et al., 2024; `merrick2024arctic`) explicitly target this zero-shot retrieval gap through large-scale contrastive pretraining, hard-negative mining, and asymmetric query instruction prefixes (`"Represent this sentence for searching relevant passages: "`).

**Analytical positioning of Archipelago:** Archipelago's frozen lexical (`bm25`, $k_1=1.5, b=0.75$) and dense (`dense_snowflake_arctic_768d`, 768-dim mean-pooled `Snowflake/snowflake-arctic-embed-m-v1.5`, L2-normalized dot product) baselines directly operationalize this classical vs. modern zero-shot contrast on a specialized computer-science corpus (Operating Systems, AI/ML, Computer Networks). Even prior to gold-label effectiveness scoring, S5-D0 technical diagnostics expose two concrete structural differences consistent with the literature:
1. **Candidate-set divergence:** `bm25` and `dense_snowflake_arctic_768d` share a mean Top-10 Jaccard similarity of only `0.4739` (Top-5: `0.4869`) and agree at Rank 1 on only `5/10` queries, with `dense` contributing `21` exclusive Top-10 pairs versus `9` for `bm25`. This confirms that lexical and dense retrieval surface materially distinct evidence pools on technical CS queries.
2. **CPU latency asymmetry:** In S5-B1.1 profiling (12 queries $\times$ 5 warm reps on CPU), `bm25` averaged `4.344 ms` warm latency (cold index build `407.279 ms`), whereas `dense_snowflake_arctic_768d` averaged `148.281 ms` warm latency (`7,630.455 ms` model load). As noted in `LIM-08`, these figures characterize a 713-passage CPU pilot and do not establish large-corpus GPU scaling.

## 2. Hybrid Retrieval and Rank Fusion: Robustness Without Calibration vs. Sensitivity to Component Quality

Because lexical and dense retrievers exhibit complementary failure modes—exact-term sensitivity in BM25 versus semantic paraphrasing and rare-term compression in dense bi-encoders—hybrid retrieval has become the standard strong baseline in modern IR. Luan et al. (2021) (`luan2021sparse`) provided a theoretical and empirical analysis showing that fixed-dimensional dense encoders face an inherent representational capacity bottleneck for long documents and exact lexical matches, and that combining sparse and dense signals systematically mitigates both weaknesses.

A central methodological question in hybrid retrieval is *how* to combine heterogeneous score distributions. Cormack, Clarke, and Büttcher (2009) (`cormack2009reciprocal`) introduced Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF), $\text{RRF}(d) = \sum_r \frac{1}{k + \text{rank}_r(d)}$ with $k=60$, demonstrating that rank-only aggregation requires no score calibration or supervised training while outperforming individual rankers and Condorcet fusion across TREC and LETOR collections. More recently, Bruch, Gai, and Ingber (2023) (`bruch2023analysis`) critically examined RRF against convex linear combinations of normalized scores across hybrid lexical+dense benchmarks. Their key methodological insight is that **RRF is sensitive to the rank-cutoff depth and discards score-magnitude margins**, making it a strong, parameter-free out-of-the-box baseline when calibration data is absent, whereas tuned convex combinations can outperform RRF when in-domain validation data is available to learn interpolation weights.

**Analytical positioning of Archipelago:** Archipelago adopts uncalibrated RRF (`hybrid_rrf_e2e`, $k=60$ over top-50 candidates from `bm25` and `dense_snowflake_arctic_768d`), which is methodologically appropriate for a zero-shot pilot with no validation split for learning convex weights (consistent with `cormack2009reciprocal` and `bruch2023analysis`). S5-D0 diagnostics confirm that `hybrid_rrf_e2e` acts as an effective bridge between the two candidate sets—sharing a mean of `7.4` Top-10 passages with `bm25` (Jaccard `0.6091`) and `7.5` with `dense` (Jaccard `0.6480`), matching `dense` at Rank 1 on `8/10` queries and `bm25` on `5/10` queries—while incurring negligible fusion overhead (`0.112–0.161 ms`, versus `95.4%–97.8%` of end-to-end latency spent in dense encoding). Crucially, however, because Archipelago evaluates only a single fixed RRF configuration ($k=60$, depth 50) without convex-combination ablations or adjudicated relevance labels, it does not test Bruch et al.'s (2023) hypothesis regarding score-aware fusion superiority.

## 3. Knowledge-Graph-Assisted and Graph-Based IR: Structural Promise vs. Extraction Sparsity and Entity-Linking Brittleness

Graph-based IR spans two distinct historical lineages that converge in contemporary retrieval-augmented systems:
- **Entity-centric and KG-embedding IR:** Xiong, Power, and Callan (2017) (`xiong2017explicit`) demonstrated in Explicit Semantic Ranking (ESR) for academic search that projecting queries and documents into a structured knowledge graph (Freebase/domain concepts) improves retrieval on concept-heavy queries where lexical matching fails. Dietz, Kotov, and Meij (2018) (`dietz2018utilizing`) synthesized the broader text-centric KG-IR paradigm, identifying two persistent bottlenecks: **entity linking / query-to-graph anchoring errors** and **incomplete graph coverage**, both of which can degrade retrieval when graph signals are noisy or sparse.
- **Graph neural and LLM-era graph retrieval:** Yasunaga et al. (2021) (`yasunaga2021qagnn`) bridged pretrained language models and knowledge graphs via joint message passing (QA-GNN), showing gains on structured reasoning tasks when relevant subgraphs are accurately retrieved. In the LLM era, Edge et al. (2024) (`edge2024local`) introduced GraphRAG, combining LLM-extracted entity graphs with hierarchical Leiden community detection and map-reduce summarization to address *global* query-focused summarization across million-token corpora—explicitly contrasting global sensemaking with local chunk retrieval. Complementarily, Jiménez Gutiérrez et al. (2024) (`gutierrez2024hipporag`) proposed HippoRAG, pairing LLM-extracted schemaless knowledge graphs with Personalized PageRank (PPR) over concept nodes to support single-step multi-hop associative retrieval across documents.

**Analytical positioning of Archipelago:** Archipelago's three graph retrievers (`baseline_graph_1hop` over 1,526 verified Kùzu edges / 451 eligible concepts; `repaired_graph_1hop_ablation` and `repaired_graph_2hop_full` over 146 corpus-scoped heterogeneous edges with typed weights `REQUIRES`, `PART_OF`, `USES`, `CONTRASTS_WITH`, `RELATED` and 2-hop decay `0.5`) occupy a deliberately lightweight, deterministic, local-passage-retrieval niche—distinct from GraphRAG's global summarization (`edge2024local`) and HippoRAG's PPR-based associative memory (`gutierrez2024hipporag`). Crucially, Archipelago's S5-D0 technical diagnostics provide concrete empirical evidence of the exact classical failure modes warned of by Dietz et al. (2018) (`dietz2018utilizing`):
1. **Graph sparsity and domain-concentrated fallback (`LIM-07`):** Because the repaired heterogeneous graph contains only 146 corpus-scoped edges (heavily concentrated in AI/ML), both `repaired_graph_1hop_ablation` and `repaired_graph_2hop_full` experience **zero anchor matches on 4/10 (40%) active queries** (all three OS queries `Q-S5B2-01, 02, 03` plus Networks negative control `Q-S5B2-11`), triggering deterministic `corpus_bm25` fallback. This mechanically inflates overall Top-10 Jaccard similarity with `bm25` to `0.4800` / `0.4852`, whereas non-fallback Jaccard with `bm25` is only `0.1333` / `0.1421`.
2. **Substring anchor-matching brittleness and slot shortfalls (`LIM-07`):** Raw substring matching (`c in query.lower()`) in `baseline_graph_1hop` caused false-positive anchor triggers—matching concept `"act"` inside the word `"exact"` on `Q-S5B2-03` and `Q-S5B2-11`—suppressing BM25 fallback while returning only 1 passage each. Combined with no partial-list backfill, `baseline_graph_1hop` suffers **31/100 missing Top-10 slots** and produces `65.2%` (`45/69`) system-exclusive pairs.
3. **Observed 2-hop traversal scope:** `repaired_graph_2hop_full` reaches observed hop depth 2 on `5/10` queries (all in AI/ML: `Q-S5B2-04`–`08`, traversing 167 hop-2 paths and altering Top-10 rankings on all 5 queries and Top-10 membership on 4/5). Whether those 2-hop rank shifts improve or degrade retrieval relevance remains strictly unknown pending S5-C2 adjudication.

## 4. Multi-Hop Retrieval and Benchmark Validity: Shortcuts, Decomposability, and Passage Necessity

A major methodological lesson from multi-hop QA and retrieval research is that **surface compositional phrasing does not guarantee genuine multi-hop necessity**. Yang et al. (2018) (`yang2018hotpotqa`) popularized explainable multi-hop evaluation with HotpotQA, pairing bridge and comparison questions with sentence-level supporting-fact annotations. However, Min et al. (2019) (`min2019compositional`) demonstrated that a large fraction of HotpotQA questions could be answered via single-hop shortcuts without composing evidence across multiple documents, showing that benchmark labels claiming "multi-hop" status must be audited for actual evidence necessity rather than assumed from question syntax.

In response, subsequent benchmarks tightened structural controls. Ho et al. (2020) (`ho2020constructing`) introduced 2WikiMultiHopQA, enforcing explicit reasoning-path provenance (entity-relation triples) to audit whether models genuinely follow multi-step chains rather than exploiting lexical shortcuts. Trivedi et al. (2022) (`trivedi2022musique`) pushed this further with MuSiQue, constructing multi-hop questions bottom-up from verified single-hop sub-questions and applying disconnected-reasoning filters to eliminate shortcut-solvable items and unanswerable-question conflation.

**Analytical positioning of Archipelago:** Archipelago's S5-B2 / S5-B2.1 dataset audit directly mirrors the diagnostic critique of Min et al. (2019) (`min2019compositional`) and the necessity discipline of Trivedi et al. (2022) (`trivedi2022musique`). When the pilot's passage-necessity matrix (`s5b2_1_passage_necessity_matrix.json`) was audited against the frozen 713-passage corpus, two queries previously tagged `CROSS_DOCUMENT` (`Q-S5B2-06` and `Q-S5B2-07`) were refuted and reclassified to `SINGLE_HOP`, while two further queries (`Q-S5A-09`, `Q-S5A-12`) were placed on `HOLD` due to truncated textbook stubs (`≤305` chars) and constructed-card provenance (`LIM-03`). As a result, **Archipelago's frozen v2.1 pilot contains zero validated cross-document queries** (`LIM-02`): only 2 queries (`Q-S5B2-02`, `Q-S5B2-10`) are same-document `TWO_HOP`, 5 are `SINGLE_HOP`, and 3 are provisional negative controls. Consequently, Archipelago cannot—and must not—claim cross-document multi-hop retrieval gains; instead, its methodological contribution on this axis is demonstrating how rigorous passage-necessity auditing prevents premature multi-hop claims on small, heterogeneous corpora.

## 5. Retrieval Evaluation Methodology: Pooling Completeness, Assessor Reliability, Statistical Power, and AI-Assisted Labeling

Rigorous IR evaluation rests on four interlocking pillars that directly govern Archipelago's S5-B2.1, S5-C2, and S5-D0 protocols:

1. **Pooling bias and incomplete judgments:** Zobel (1998) (`zobel1998reliable`) established that while TREC-style depth pooling preserves relative system orderings reasonably well, incomplete pools systematically overestimate absolute recall and can bias against unpooled or structurally distinct systems. Buckley and Voorhees (2004) (`buckley2004retrieval`) formalized this vulnerability under incomplete relevance information, introducing `bpref` and showing that standard metrics degrade sharply when unjudged retrieved documents are silently treated as non-relevant. Archipelago directly addresses this risk in S5-B2.1 by abandoning its initial 25-pair seed pool (which covered only `7.0%–14.0%` of Top-10 outputs) in favor of **complete depth-10 union pooling across all 6 frozen systems plus seed pairs** (`254` unique pairs: `244` retrieved union + `10` seed-only), achieving `100.0%` Top-5 and Top-10 pool coverage and enforcing a strict validation error in `evaluate_gold_labels_readonly.py` if any Top-10 passage lacks a gold judgment.
2. **Graded relevance and inter-annotator agreement:** Järvelin and Kekäläinen (2002) (`jarvelin2002cumulated`) introduced Discounted Cumulated Gain (DCG / nDCG) to evaluate graded relevance scales with rank-position discounting, which Archipelago implements via exponential gain $2^g - 1$ over its 3-level scale (`0`, `1`, `2`). Voorhees (2000) (`voorhees2000variations`) showed that human assessors frequently disagree on individual relevance judgments, yet relative system rankings remain stable on large TREC query sets—an invariance that *cannot* be assumed on a 10-query pilot. To quantify ordinal agreement across graded judgments, Cohen (1968) (`cohen1968weighted`) introduced weighted kappa ($\kappa_w$), which penalizes extreme disagreements (`0` vs. `2`) more heavily than adjacent ones (`1` vs. `2`). Archipelago reports both unweighted $\kappa = 0.5972$ and quadratic weighted $\kappa = 0.7677$ (203/254 exact agreements; 51 disagreements) strictly as descriptive pre-adjudication diagnostics.
3. **Sample size and statistical power:** Sakai (2016) (`sakai2016statistical`) conducted a systematic review of a decade of SIGIR and TOIS papers (2006–2015), demonstrating that underpowered query samples routinely produce unreliable significance tests and inflated Type II / Type M error rates, and recommending formal power and sample-size analysis (typically $\ge 50$ topics for moderate effect sizes in ad-hoc IR). With only **10 active queries** (7 answerable + 3 provisional negative controls; `LIM-01`), Archipelago is explicitly below statistical-testing thresholds; accordingly, S5-D0 restricts planned post-adjudication reporting to descriptive pilot metrics without significance claims.
4. **LLM-assisted relevance judgments and independence:** Faggioli et al. (2023) (`faggioli2023perspectives`) systematically analyzed the promise and methodological hazards of using Large Language Models for relevance judgments, warning against conflating AI-assisted or AI-generated labels with independent human ground truth due to circularity, anchoring bias, and unvalidated alignment on specialized tasks. This directly underpins Archipelago's S5-C2 governance decision (`LIM-05`, `LIM-06`): because Reviewer B disclosed `AI-assisted proposed annotation; requires independent human confirmation` on all `254/254` pairs and 51 disagreements remain unadjudicated, S5-C2 rightly placed final evaluation on hold (`HOLD_FOR_DISAGREEMENT_ADJUDICATION_AND_REV_B_INDEPENDENCE_CHECK`) rather than treating Reviewer B's labels as validated human ground truth.
