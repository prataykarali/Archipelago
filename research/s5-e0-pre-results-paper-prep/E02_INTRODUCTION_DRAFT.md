## 1. Introduction

Retrieving grounding evidence from technical academic corpora—such as computer science textbooks, systems lectures, and peer-reviewed research papers—poses a persistent tension for information retrieval (IR) systems. Technical queries combine exact domain terminology, mathematical identifiers, and acronyms with conceptual relationships across definitions, mechanisms, and architectural tradeoffs. Practitioners and researchers increasingly choose among four retrieval paradigms for such corpora: probabilistic lexical matching (BM25), zero-shot dense bi-encoder embeddings, hybrid rank fusion, and knowledge-graph-assisted traversal. Yet controlled comparisons across all four paradigms on the same frozen technical corpus remain rare, and methodological lapses in corpus curation, information-access parity, pooling completeness, or annotation provenance can easily obscure whether observed behavior reflects genuine retrieval properties or experimental artifacts.

Existing literature establishes well-characterized strengths and blind spots for each paradigm, leaving concrete diagnostic questions unresolved in small, heterogeneous technical collections. Probabilistic lexical models such as Okapi BM25 (`robertson1994some`, `robertson2009probabilistic`, `trotman2014improvements`) provide strong, zero-training exact-term matching and remain difficult to beat consistently in out-of-domain technical search (`thakur2021beir`), but fail when relevant passages paraphrase query terminology. Siamese and dual-encoder dense retrievers (`reimers2019sentence`, `karpukhin2020dense`), including modern instruction-prefixed models such as Snowflake Arctic-Embed (`merrick2024arctic`), capture semantic similarity without lexical overlap, yet face fixed-dimensional representational limits on exact identifiers (`luan2021sparse`) and substantial CPU query-encoding latency relative to inverted indices. Hybrid fusion via parameter-free Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF; `cormack2009reciprocal`) mitigates score-scale incompatibility without calibration data, though recent analyses show that RRF discards score margins and remains sensitive to rank cutoffs (`bruch2023analysis`).

Knowledge-graph-assisted retrieval offers an appealing structural complement by linking explicit concepts through typed relations (`xiong2017explicit`, `yasunaga2021qagnn`). Contemporary LLM-era graph frameworks target either global query-focused summarization over hierarchical community summaries (GraphRAG; `edge2024local`) or multi-hop associative recall via Personalized PageRank over extracted triples (HippoRAG; `gutierrez2024hipporag`). However, classical text-centric KG-IR research cautions that graph retrieval is highly vulnerable to entity-linking errors and incomplete graph coverage (`dietz2018utilizing`). Moreover, multi-hop evaluation studies demonstrate that surface compositional phrasing does not guarantee multi-hop necessity: benchmark audits have repeatedly uncovered single-hop shortcuts (`min2019compositional`) and motivated explicit reasoning-path and sub-question necessity verification (`yang2018hotpotqa`, `ho2020constructing`, `trivedi2022musique`). Finally, IR evaluation methodology underscores that incomplete judgment pools bias recall (`zobel1998reliable`, `buckley2004retrieval`), small query samples lack statistical power for significance testing (`sakai2016statistical`), assessor disagreements require explicit modeling and adjudication (`cohen1968weighted`, `voorhees2000variations`), and AI-assisted relevance labels cannot be equated with independent human ground truth (`faggioli2023perspectives`).

These converging methodological risks motivate an **auditable, parity-enforced pilot study** before scaling to a larger multi-domain benchmark. In this paper, we examine six frozen retrieval configurations within the Archipelago testbed over a cryptographically locked technical corpus (`s5a_eval_corpus_v1`: 713 passages across 53 computer science documents spanning Operating Systems, AI/ML, and Computer Networks): (1) lexical `bm25` (`BM25Okapi`, $k_1=1.5, b=0.75$); (2) zero-shot dense bi-encoder `dense_snowflake_arctic_768d` (`Snowflake/snowflake-arctic-embed-m-v1.5`, 768-dim, CPU); (3) hybrid rank fusion `hybrid_rrf_e2e` (RRF, $k=60$ over top-50 lexical and dense lists); (4) corpus-scoped 1-hop Kùzu graph retrieval `baseline_graph_1hop` (1,526 verified edges across 451 eligible concepts); (5) repaired heterogeneous 1-hop graph retrieval `repaired_graph_1hop_ablation` (146 corpus-scoped typed edges); and (6) repaired heterogeneous 2-hop graph retrieval `repaired_graph_2hop_full` (identical 146-edge graph with multiplicative hop-2 decay `0.5`). All six systems operate under strict 713-passage information-access parity with deterministic tie-breaking, canonical `{doc_id}::{chunk_id}` identifiers, and runtime SHA-256 verification.

Rather than assuming benchmark validity or rushing to headline effectiveness numbers, we enforced a staged integrity audit prior to relevance scoring. Corpus and query-necessity auditing placed 2 of 12 original queries on `HOLD` due to truncated textbook stubs (`Q-S5A-09`) and constructed-card provenance (`Q-S5A-12`), and refuted cross-document labels on `Q-S5B2-06` and `Q-S5B2-07` (reclassifying both to `SINGLE_HOP`), leaving **10 active queries** (7 answerable: 5 `SINGLE_HOP` and 2 same-document `TWO_HOP`; plus 3 provisional negative controls) and **zero validated cross-document queries**. To eliminate pooling bias across the six systems, we expanded candidate assessment from a 25-pair seed set (7.0%–14.0% Top-10 coverage) to a complete depth-10 blinded union pool of **254 unique `(query_id, passage_id)` pairs** (100.0% Top-5 and Top-10 coverage). During pre-adjudication annotation auditing (Stage S5-C2), both reviewers submitted 254/254 structurally valid annotations (203/254 exact agreements; 51 disagreements; descriptive quadratic weighted $\kappa = 0.7677$), but Reviewer B disclosed AI-assisted proposed annotations on all 254 pairs and human negative-control verification remained incomplete. In accordance with governance standards for LLM-assisted judgments (`faggioli2023perspectives`), final relevance scoring is held (`HOLD`) pending independent human confirmation of Reviewer B, adjudication of all 51 disagreements, and human verification of the three negative controls.

### Research Questions

To maintain a strict boundary between verified system diagnostics and blocked relevance effectiveness, this pilot addresses three research questions:

- **RQ1 (Cross-Paradigm Descriptive Effectiveness — Blocked Pending Adjudication):** Once independently validated human gold judgments become available, how do the six parity-enforced retrieval configurations compare in descriptive relevance effectiveness (`Recall@5/10`, `Precision@5/10`, `MRR@10`, and graded `nDCG@5/10` per `jarvelin2002cumulated`) on verified answerable queries, and how do they behave on verified negative controls?
- **RQ2 (Anchor Coverage, Substring Matching, and Fallback Mechanics):** How do graph anchor coverage, raw substring matching, and deterministic `corpus_bm25` fallback affect observable retrieval behavior (candidate overlap, system exclusivity, and Top-10 slot completeness) and constrain cross-paradigm comparisons?
- **RQ3 (One-Hop vs. Two-Hop Traversal Perturbation):** What measurable changes in observed hop depth, traversed paths, candidate set membership, and Top-K ranking order arise when extending deterministic heterogeneous graph retrieval from one hop to two hops, independent of unverified relevance impact?

### Scoped Contributions

Within the strict boundaries of a 10-query pilot, this work makes the following contributions:

1. **An Auditable, Parity-Enforced Six-System Pilot Protocol:** We document a cryptographically locked 713-passage corpus (`s5a_eval_corpus_v1`), a 4-tier passage provenance registry, strict graph information-access parity (excluding 462 unscoped Kùzu edges and 2 external heterogeneous edges), complete depth-10 blinded pooling (254 pairs, 100.0% Top-10 coverage), and a gate-enforced read-only evaluation pipeline (`evaluate_gold_labels_readonly.py`) that refuses silent zero-imputation of unjudged passages (`buckley2004retrieval`).
2. **Empirical Relevance-Agnostic Diagnostics of Graph Retrieval Mechanics:** Prior to gold-label scoring, we quantify two concrete failure modes that materially affect cross-paradigm interpretation: (a) domain-concentrated graph sparsity causing **4/10 (40.0%)** `corpus_bm25` fallback in `repaired_graph_*` (mechanically inflating overall Top-10 Jaccard with `bm25` from `0.1333`/`0.1421` on non-fallback queries to `0.4800`/`0.4852` overall), and (b) raw substring anchor false positives (`"act"` inside `"exact"` on `Q-S5B2-03` and `Q-S5B2-11`) paired with no partial-list backfill, causing **31/100 missing Top-10 slots** and **65.2%** (`45/69`) exclusive pairs in `baseline_graph_1hop`. We further document that 2-hop traversal activates on **5/10** queries (all AI/ML), altering Top-10 rank order on 5/5 and set membership on 4/5 without presuming relevance gain.
3. **Methodological Negative Findings on Multi-Hop Necessity and Annotation Governance:** We demonstrate through explicit passage-necessity auditing (`min2019compositional`) that ostensibly cross-document queries in an initial pilot collapsed to single-hop evidence (leaving 0 validated cross-document queries), and we document a reproducible governance gate that halts effectiveness reporting when AI-assisted annotation provenance (`faggioli2023perspectives`) and 51/254 disagreements remain unresolved.

### Explicit Pilot-Study Limitations

This study is intentionally scoped as an exploratory diagnostic pilot rather than a definitive benchmark comparison. Four structural constraints govern all interpretations: (i) the active query set (`N = 10`, with `7` answerable queries across three CS domains) cannot support statistical significance testing, system superiority claims, or broad domain generalization (`sakai2016statistical`); (ii) because **zero validated cross-document queries** remain, no cross-document multi-hop retrieval claims can be made; (iii) corpus provenance auditing identified 21 truncated textbook stubs ($\le 305$ chars), 1 constructed pilot card, and sparse OSTEP chapter extraction in the frozen corpus; and (iv) multi-repetition warm CPU latencies were profiled in S5-B1.1 on the pre-revision 12-query set over 713 passages and do not establish large-corpus or GPU scaling behavior. `[TODO — BLOCKED ON S5-C3 / S5-D1: Update annotation-hold status once S5-C3 adjudication and S5-D1 scoring are finalized.]`
